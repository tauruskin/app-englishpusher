📢 Giving & Responding to News

Unit 5C · 27 questions · 10 cards

Gap Fill

___ what! I passed my driving test first time!

Answer: Guess

Guess what! always comes before good news, never bad news.

Bad news, I'm ___. The flight's been delayed.

Answer: afraid

Giving bad news: I'm afraid. Responding to bad news: I'm sorry.

You won't believe ___, but Maria got engaged yesterday!

Answer: this

This phrase works for both good and bad news — the 'but' leads into either.

My brother ___ a new job, so he's moving to London next month.

Answer: got

Get a new job, get promoted, get engaged — all use 'get', not 'make' or 'do'.

Wow, congratulations! I'm so pleased ___ you.

Answer: for

I'm pleased/happy for you — always 'for', never 'with' or 'about'.

What a ___! I'm really sorry you lost the match.

Answer: shame

What a shame! = That's a pity! Use it to show sympathy.

Error Spot

Guess that! I finally passed my driving test.

Correction: what

Always 'Guess what!', never 'Guess that' or 'Guess this'.

I've got some good news for tell you: I'm moving to Spain.

Correction: to

News to tell you — infinitive 'to', not 'for'.

Bad news, I'm sorry. I lost my phone again.

Correction: afraid

Giving your own bad news: I'm afraid. Not I'm sorry.

I'm so relieved - I finally won my driving test this time!

Correction: passed

Pass/fail a test; win a competition or match — don't mix them up.

My sister made a baby last month, so now I'm an uncle.

Correction: had

Have a baby, not make a baby.

My cousin's getting marriage next summer - she's so excited!

Correction: married

Get married (adjective), not get marriage (noun).

Multiple Choice

A friend just told you: 'I passed my driving test!' Which is the best response?

Answer: Congratulations! Well done!

Congratulations! / Well done! — the classic pair of responses to good news.

You want to introduce some exciting news to a friend. Which phrase fits best?

Answer: Guess what! I've got some amazing news.

Guess what! + news statement = classic opener for good news.

You need to tell a colleague something disappointing. Which phrase introduces it best?

Answer: Bad news, I'm afraid.

Bad news, I'm afraid — a soft way to introduce bad news.

A colleague says: 'I'm so sorry, I failed my exam again.' Which response fits best?

Answer: What a shame. I'm really sorry to hear that.

What a shame / I'm sorry to hear that — sympathetic responses to bad news.

Tom asked for more responsibility, and his boss gave him a higher position at the same company. Which sentence describes this correctly?

Answer: He got promoted.

Promoted = higher role, same company. New job = different job.

You're not sure if your friend will think your news is good or bad. Which opener fits best?

Answer: You won't believe this, but...

Use this opener when you're not sure how the news will be received.

A friend tells you their grandmother is in hospital. Which is the correct sympathetic response?

Answer: I'm so sorry to hear that.

Sympathy for their news: I'm sorry to hear that. Apology for your own fault: I'm sorry I...

Word Transform

He felt so proud when he finally ___ (pass) his driving test last week.

Answer: passed

Pass → passed (regular -ed past simple).

I'm so excited - I've just ___ (win) two tickets to the festival!

Answer: won

Win → won (irregular past participle), not 'winned'.

My sister ___ (have) a baby next spring - she's due in April.

Answer: is having

Present continuous for fixed future plans: She is having a baby in April.

My neighbours got ___ (engage) last weekend after years together.

Answer: engaged

Get engaged, get married — 'get' + past participle adjective.

I've just ___ (lose) my job, so I'm updating my CV tonight.

Answer: lost

Lose → lost (irregular). I've lost my job.

We're all really ___ (excite) about Maria's new baby.

Answer: excited

Excited (how you feel) vs exciting (what causes the feeling).

Sentence Match

Match each way of giving news to its correct ending:

Guess → what!

I've got some good news → to tell you.

Bad news, → I'm afraid.

You won't believe → this, but...

Learn these openers as whole chunks, not word by word.

Match each response to news to its correct ending:

I'm so pleased → for you.

That's wonderful → news!

What a → shame.

I'm really sorry → to hear that.

'I'm so pleased for you', 'That's wonderful news!', 'What a shame' and 'I'm really sorry to hear that' are all fixed phrases.

Flashcards

Guess what! (giving good news)

___ what! I've just been offered a new job!

Back: Guess what! I've just been offered a new job! Answer as Guess

Guess what! is a fixed phrase used to get someone's attention before sharing exciting news. It always introduces good news, never bad.

Guess what! My sister just had a baby boy. · Guess what! We won the quiz last night.

«Вгадай що!» — фраза, щоб почати гарну новину: Guess what!

Bad news, I'm afraid (giving bad news)

Bad news, I'm ___ — the match was cancelled.

Back: Bad news, I'm afraid — the match was cancelled. Answer as afraid

We use I'm afraid to softly introduce our own bad news. I'm sorry is for responding to someone else's bad news, not for announcing your own.

Bad news, I'm afraid - the shop is closed today. · I'm afraid the meeting has been moved.

Повідомляємо погану новину — I'm afraid, не I'm sorry (те для відповіді).

You won't believe this, but... (good or bad news)

You won't believe ___, but Tom's getting married!

Back: You won't believe this, but Tom's getting married! Answer as this

This fixed phrase can introduce either good or bad news. It builds a little suspense before the main point.

You won't believe this, but I lost my phone again. · You won't believe this, but we won the lottery!

«Ти не повіриш, але...» підходить і для гарних, і для поганих новин.

pleased to tell you (giving good news formally)

We're really ___ (please) to tell you that you got the scholarship.

Back: We're really pleased to tell you that you got the scholarship. Answer as pleased

Pleased to tell you... introduces good news warmly or formally. Pleased is an adjective here, describing how the speaker feels.

I'm pleased to tell you the results were excellent. · We're pleased to tell you your order has arrived.

«Радий повідомити» — I'm pleased to tell you... (pleased — прикметник).

Congratulations! (celebrating good news)

She just got engaged - ___!

Back: She just got engaged - Congratulations! Answer as Congratulations

Congratulations celebrates any happy event, such as an engagement, a baby or a birthday. Well done! is specifically for something a person worked hard to achieve, like passing a test.

Congratulations on your new house! · Well done on finishing the marathon!

Congratulations — для будь-якої приємної події; Well done — за досягнення.

pleased for you (preposition for)

I just heard your good news. I'm so pleased ___ you.

Back: I just heard your good news. I'm so pleased for you. Answer as for

Pleased for someone means happy because of their good fortune. The preposition is always for, never with or about.

I'm so pleased for you - you deserve it! · We're really pleased for Anna and her new job.

«Я радий за тебе» — pleased for you (завжди for).

What a shame (responding to bad news)

What a ___! I'm sorry you didn't get the part in the play.

Back: What a shame! I'm sorry you didn't get the part in the play. Answer as shame

What a shame and I'm sorry to hear that both respond sympathetically to bad news. Shame here means disappointment, not embarrassment.

What a shame you missed the party! · I'm sorry to hear that your flight was cancelled.

«Як шкода» — What a shame (shame тут = прикрість, не сором).

get married (adjective, not the noun marriage)

My uncle is getting ___ (marry) in the autumn.

Back: My uncle is getting married in the autumn. Answer as married

Married is the adjective used after get and be. Marriage is a noun and cannot follow getting directly.

My cousin got engaged last weekend. · They got married in a small church.

Після get — married (прикметник), не marriage (іменник).

pass an exam/test (not win)

It took him three tries, but he finally ___ (pass) his theory exam.

Back: It took him three tries, but he finally passed his theory exam. Answer as passed

We pass or fail exams and tests. Win is only used for competitions, matches or prizes - never for exams.

She passed all her university exams. · He failed the test twice before passing.

Іспити/тести — pass або fail, а не win (win лише для змагань).

have a baby (fixed collocation)

My best friend ___ (have) twins in March, and I can't wait to meet them!

Back: My best friend had twins in March, and I can't wait to meet them! Answer as had

Have a baby is the fixed phrase for giving birth. We don't say make or get a baby.

They had a baby girl in the spring. · My neighbours just had twins!

«Народити дитину» — have a baby (не make чи get).
