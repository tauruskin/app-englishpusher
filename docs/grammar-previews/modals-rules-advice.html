🚦 Modals for Rules and Advice

Lesson 4A — 25 questions · 8 cards

Gap Fill

You ___ drink and drive. It's extremely dangerous and against the law.

Answer: mustn't

mustn't = forbidden, no choice. don't have to / needn't = not necessary, but still allowed.

You ___ pay to enter the museum on Sundays - it's free for everyone.

Answer: don't have to

don't have to = optional, your choice. mustn't = forbidden, no choice.

I ___ wear a uniform at work. It's the company's rule, not my choice.

Answer: have to

have to = someone else's rule. must = my own decision or feeling.

I ___ call my mum tonight - I promised her I would.

Answer: must

must = I think it's necessary. have to = a rule says it's necessary.

You ___ try the new Italian restaurant on Main Street - the food is amazing.

Answer: should

should = good idea (advice). must / have to = necessary (obligation).

You ___ bring any food to the picnic - we've already bought enough for everyone.

Answer: needn't

needn't = don't have to (not necessary). Both are the opposite of must / have to.

Error Spot

She must leaves now because the meeting starts in five minutes.

Correction: leave

After 'must', use the base form: 'must leave', not 'must leaves'.

He doesn't has to work on Saturdays this month.

Correction: have

'Doesn't' already has the -s, so use the base form: 'doesn't have to', not 'doesn't has to'.

You mustn't worrying about the exam. You have prepared really well.

Correction: worry

After 'mustn't', use the base form: 'mustn't worry', not 'mustn't worrying'.

We needn't buying tickets in advance - we can pay at the door.

Correction: buy

After 'needn't', use the base form: 'needn't buy', not 'needn't buying'.

She should calls her landlord about the broken heater.

Correction: call

After 'should', use the base form: 'should call', not 'should calls'.

Does she has to finish the project by Monday?

Correction: have

'Does' already has the -s, so use the base form: 'Does she have to...?', not 'Does she has to...?'

Multiple Choice

Which sentence means it is not allowed?

Answer: You mustn't park here - it's a fire exit.

mustn't = forbidden. don't have to / needn't = not necessary, but still allowed.

Which sentence describes an obligation set by someone else?

Answer: Employees have to wear safety helmets on the building site.

have to = someone else's rule. must = my own decision or feeling.

Which sentence expresses a lack of obligation, not a prohibition?

Answer: You don't have to answer every question - just do your best.

don't have to = optional. mustn't = forbidden. Never confuse them!

Choose the sentence with the same meaning.

Answer: You needn't pay now.

needn't ≈ don't have to (not necessary). Different from mustn't (forbidden).

Which sentence is advice, not a strict rule?

Answer: You should take a painkiller and rest for a while.

should = friendly advice. must / have to = obligation, no choice.

Which sentence is advice against something, rather than a prohibition?

Answer: You shouldn't eat so much junk food - it's not good for you.

shouldn't = bad idea (advice). mustn't = forbidden (no choice).

Which sentence correctly says that something is necessary?

Answer: You need to renew your passport before it expires.

need to = necessary. needn't = NOT necessary. One suffix apart, opposite meanings!

Word Transform

She ___ (have) to finish her homework before dinner every day.

Answer: has

have to → has to for he/she/it. Real modals (must, should) never add -s.

He ___ (need) to see a doctor about his back.

Answer: needs

need to → needs to for he/she/it, just like have to → has to.

We ___ (have) to wait two hours at the airport yesterday because of a delay.

Answer: had

must → had to in the past. There is no 'musted'!

You ___ (have) to show your passport at the border tomorrow.

Answer: will have

Future obligation → will have to, never 'will must'.

I ___ (have) to work this weekend. My boss said I can take the days off.

Answer: don't have

don't have to = optional, your choice. mustn't = forbidden, no choice.

You ___ (need) worry about the interview. You're well prepared.

Answer: don't need to

don't need to = needn't = not necessary. Never confuse with mustn't (forbidden).

Flashcards

must — strong obligation (what I think is necessary)

I ___ finish this project tonight - I promised my boss. as ___

Back: I must finish this project tonight - I promised my boss., answer as must

Must expresses something the speaker feels is necessary or important themselves, not a rule made by someone else. It often sounds more personal and urgent than have to.

I must remember to call the dentist today. · You must try this cake - it's amazing!

must — особисте відчуття обов'язку: «я мушу», не зовнішнє правило.

mustn't — prohibition (not allowed)

You ___ touch that wire - it's extremely dangerous. as ___

Back: You mustn't touch that wire - it's extremely dangerous., answer as mustn't

Mustn't means something is forbidden or not allowed at all - there is no choice. It is very different from don't have to, which means something is simply not necessary.

Passengers mustn't open the doors while the train is moving. · You mustn't tell anyone my secret.

mustn't — заборонено: «не можна», а не просто «не обов'язково».

have to — obligation from outside (a rule, not your choice)

In this office, staff ___ clock in before nine. as ___

Back: In this office, staff have to clock in before nine., answer as have to

Have to describes an obligation that comes from somewhere else - a law, a workplace, a school. It feels less personal than must, which usually expresses the speaker's own opinion.

In Japan, you have to take your shoes off before entering a house. · Drivers have to stop at a red light.

have to — обов'язок ззовні (закон, правило), не особиста думка.

don't have to — no obligation (it's your choice)

You ___ wear a suit to the interview - smart casual is fine. as ___

Back: You don't have to wear a suit to the interview - smart casual is fine., answer as don't have to

Don't have to means something isn't necessary, but you're still allowed to do it if you want. This is completely different from mustn't, which means it isn't allowed at all.

You don't have to come to the party if you're tired. · She doesn't have to work on Fridays this semester.

don't have to — не обов'язково (можна, якщо хочеш), на відміну від mustn't.

need to — necessity (similar to have to)

We ___ leave soon if we want to catch the train. as ___

Back: We need to leave soon if we want to catch the train., answer as need to

Need to expresses necessity, very similarly to have to. It can describe obligations from outside or things the speaker feels are important.

I need to buy some milk on the way home. · Does she need to sign the form today?

need to ≈ have to — потрібно, необхідно.

needn't / don't need to — not necessary

You ___ bring an umbrella - the forecast says it won't rain. as ___

Back: You needn't bring an umbrella - the forecast says it won't rain., answer as needn't

Needn't and don't need to both mean something isn't necessary. They have almost the same meaning as don't have to.

You needn't apologise - it wasn't your fault. · We don't need to hurry; we have plenty of time.

needn't ≈ don't need to ≈ don't have to — не обов'язково.

should — giving advice (a good idea)

You ___ drink more water in this hot weather. as ___

Back: You should drink more water in this hot weather., answer as should

Should gives advice or a recommendation. It suggests something is a good idea, but the person is free to decide for themselves.

You should see a dentist about that tooth. · We should leave earlier next time to avoid the traffic.

should — порада, гарна ідея, але не обов'язок.

shouldn't — advice against (not a good idea)

You ___ stay up so late before an exam. as ___

Back: You shouldn't stay up so late before an exam., answer as shouldn't

Shouldn't means something is not a good idea - it's advice against doing it. It's much weaker than mustn't, which means something is forbidden.

You shouldn't believe everything you read online. · He shouldn't drive if he's that tired.

shouldn't — погана ідея (порада), а не заборона, як mustn't.
