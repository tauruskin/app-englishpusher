🪞 Reflexive Pronouns

Unit 7D · 25 questions · 8 cards

Gap Fill

I hurt ___ while I was chopping vegetables for dinner.

Answer: myself

Subject and object are the same person → use a reflexive pronoun: myself, yourself, himself...

They introduced ___ to the new manager before the meeting started.

Answer: themselves

They/we introduce themselves/ourselves — plural subject, plural reflexive pronoun.

She always prefers travelling by ___ rather than in a big group.

Answer: herself

By myself/herself/himself = alone, with no company.

We treated ___ to a big breakfast after the long hike.

Answer: ourselves

We → ourselves. Never 'we' + 'themselves' or 'yourselves'.

Enjoy ___ at the party tonight, Tom!

Answer: yourself

Enjoy yourself! (one person) / Enjoy yourselves! (more than one person).

Mark says he painted the entire fence ___ last summer, without hiring anyone.

Answer: himself

A reflexive pronoun at the end of a sentence emphasises 'alone, with no help'.

Error Spot

The light just switched herself on when I opened the door.

Correction: itself

Objects and animals never take 'himself' or 'herself' — always 'itself'.

The kids managed to tidy their room by theirself before the guests arrived.

Correction: themselves

There is no word 'theirself' — the correct plural form is 'themselves'.

I can email yourself the report as soon as it's ready.

Correction: you

Reflexive pronouns are only for when the subject and object are the SAME person.

We're planning to drive to the coast yourselves this weekend.

Correction: ourselves

Match the reflexive pronoun to the subject: we → ourselves.

Jake was really proud that he'd fixed the old radio by itself without any help.

Correction: himself

People always take himself/herself/myself/yourself — never 'itself'.

Maria painted the whole fence by yourself last weekend.

Correction: herself

Check who the subject is before choosing the reflexive pronoun.

Multiple Choice

Which sentence means the person did the task completely alone?

Answer: She painted the room by herself.

By + myself/herself/himself = alone.

Which sentence correctly offers something for free to a guest?

Answer: Help yourself to some cookies.

Help yourself! = take some, it's free.

Which sentence correctly describes a machine switching off on its own?

Answer: The oven turns itself off after an hour.

Objects and machines always take 'itself'.

Which sentence correctly describes a group of people who did something without help?

Answer: My grandparents built this house themselves in the 1960s.

A plural subject referring to other people takes 'themselves'.

Which sentence correctly emphasises that nobody helped her?

Answer: She did it all herself.

A reflexive pronoun at the end of a sentence = emphasis on 'alone, no help'.

You want the person across the table to pass you the salt. Which sentence is correct?

Answer: Could you pass me the salt, please?

Different people as subject and object → use an object pronoun, not a reflexive one.

Tom and Ana are standing in front of a large mirror, both smiling at their own reflections. Which sentence describes the photo correctly?

Answer: They are looking at themselves in the mirror.

Themselves = your own reflection/action. Each other = one person and another person.

Word Transform

I cut ___ (I) while chopping vegetables for dinner.

Answer: myself

I → myself.

The twins dressed ___ (they) for the school play without any help.

Answer: themselves

They → themselves.

This old printer still switches ___ (it) off if it overheats.

Answer: itself

It → itself (always, for things and animals).

Did you two manage to fix the shelf ___ (you), or did you call someone?

Answer: yourselves

You (one person) → yourself. You (more than one person) → yourselves.

He's usually shy, but he's really enjoying ___ (he) at the party tonight.

Answer: himself

He → himself. Enjoy + reflexive pronoun = have a good time.

Although no one was around, Sarah convinced ___ (she) to keep practising the piano.

Answer: herself

She → herself, when the subject convinces/tells/reminds... themselves.

Flashcards

reflexive pronoun as the object of the verb

I cut ___ while I was shaving this morning.

Back: I cut myself while I was shaving this morning., answer as myself

We use a reflexive pronoun when the subject and the object of the verb are the same person. The pronoun comes right after the verb (or a preposition).

I burned myself on the hot pan. · They hurt themselves playing football.

myself/himself... — коли підмет і додаток — та сама особа.

by + reflexive pronoun = alone

He decided to go travelling across Asia by ___ for six months.

Back: He decided to go travelling across Asia by himself for six months., answer as himself

'By' + a reflexive pronoun means 'alone', with no one else's help or company.

She lives by herself in a small flat. · They prefer working by themselves without interruptions.

by + myself/himself... = «сам, без інших» (один).

fixed expressions: Enjoy/Help/Behave yourself!

Help ___ to some more cake — there's plenty left!

Back: Help yourself to some more cake — there's plenty left!, answer as yourself

Enjoy yourself!, Help yourself! and Behave yourself! are fixed expressions. 'Help yourself' means take some, it's free.

Enjoy yourself at the party tonight! · Behave yourself — we're in a restaurant!

Help yourself! = «пригощайся!» (візьми безкоштовно).

reflexive pronoun for emphasis

Nobody helped me — I fixed the leaking tap ___.

Back: Nobody helped me — I fixed the leaking tap myself., answer as myself

We can add a reflexive pronoun at the end of a sentence to emphasise that the subject did something alone, without help.

I made this cake myself. · Did you write the whole report yourself?

У кінці речення myself/himself... підкреслює: «сам, без сторонньої допомоги».

itself for things and animals

This old printer still switches ___ off if it overheats.

Back: This old printer still switches itself off if it overheats., answer as itself

Use 'itself' for things and animals — English nouns don't have grammatical gender, so we never use 'himself' or 'herself' for an object.

The dog taught itself a new trick. · The security light turns itself on at night.

itself — для речей і тварин: без роду, ніколи himself/herself.

plural subjects: ourselves, yourselves, themselves

My parents built this garden shed ___ last summer.

Back: My parents built this garden shed themselves last summer., answer as themselves

Plural subjects (we, you [plural], they) take the plural reflexive pronouns ourselves, yourselves and themselves.

We organised the whole trip ourselves. · Did you two paint the fence yourselves?

Множина: we→ourselves, you(мн.)→yourselves, they→themselves.

don't use a reflexive pronoun for a different person

Could you call ___ (I) later today?

Back: Could you call me later today?, answer as me

If the object of the verb is a different person from the subject, use an object pronoun (me, you, him...), not a reflexive pronoun.

She told him to wait outside. · Can you pass me the salt, please?

Якщо об'єкт — інша особа, а не підмет, вживай me/him/her, а не -self.

themselves ≠ each other (reflexive vs reciprocal)

The twins admired ___ in the mirror before leaving the house.

Back: The twins admired themselves in the mirror before leaving the house., answer as themselves

'Themselves' means each person looked at their own reflection. 'Each other' would mean they looked at one another instead — a different meaning.

She looked at herself in the shop window. · The two friends hugged each other at the airport.

themselves = кожен на себе; each other = одне на одного (інше значення).
