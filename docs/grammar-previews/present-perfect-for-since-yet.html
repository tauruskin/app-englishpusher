⏳ Present Perfect: For, Since and Yet

Lesson 6D — 25 questions · 6 cards

Gap Fill

I've lived in this town ___ I was born.

Answer: since

since + a point in time or a clause (since 2016 / since I was born) — not a length of time.

We haven't seen each other ___ a few months.

Answer: for

for + a length of time (for two years / for a few months). since + a point in time.

Has the bus arrived ___?

Answer: yet

Yet in questions = has it happened by now? It goes at the end: Have you finished yet?

My parents have run their own shop ___ 2015.

Answer: since

since + a specific point (a year, a day, an event): since 2015, since Monday, since the meeting.

I haven't tried that new café ___.

Answer: yet

haven't/hasn't + past participle + yet = it hasn't happened, but it probably will.

They've been waiting ___ three hours, and the delivery still hasn't arrived.

Answer: for

for + a number of hours/days/years. During + a specific event or period (during the meeting), not a duration.

Error Spot

I've worked here for 2020.

Correction: since

'2020' is a point in time → use 'since': 'I've worked here since 2020.'

She has lived in Spain since eight years.

Correction: for

'Eight years' is a duration → use 'for': 'She has lived in Spain for eight years.'

She have lived in Tokyo since 2018.

Correction: has

Third person singular needs 'has': 'She has lived in Tokyo since 2018.'

I have yet eaten lunch.

Correction: already

This is an affirmative sentence → use 'already': 'I have already eaten lunch.'

We haven't visited our grandparents for last month.

Correction: since

'Last month' is a point in time → use 'since': 'We haven't visited our grandparents since last month.'

They haven't finished the project already.

Correction: yet

Negative sentences use 'yet', not 'already': 'They haven't finished the project yet.'

Multiple Choice

Choose the sentence that correctly uses 'for':

Answer: I've worked at this school for six years.

for + duration (for six years). since + point (since 2015). ago + past simple (six years ago).

Choose the sentence that correctly uses 'since':

Answer: He's played the guitar since he was ten.

since + clause (since I was ten) or since + point (since 2015) — never since + a length of time.

Choose the sentence that correctly uses 'yet':

Answer: Have you called the bank yet?

yet → questions & negatives. already → affirmative sentences.

My sister has lived in Berlin since 2020.

Answer: She moved to Berlin in 2020 and still lives there now.

have/has + past participle + since + point = started then, still true now.

Find the sentence with an error:

Answer: We haven't seen that film since.

since always needs a point after it (since Monday / since I was young). Yet can stand alone at the end.

Choose the sentence with the correct position of 'yet':

Answer: I haven't finished packing yet.

Put yet at the very end: I haven't finished yet. Have you called yet?

Choose the correct sentence:

Answer: I haven't been to Italy for years, but I've always wanted to go.

for + a length of time, even vague ones like 'for years', 'for ages', 'for a while'.

Word Transform

She ___ (live) in this house since she was born.

Answer: has lived

has/have + past participle + since + point = state started then, still true now.

We ___ (own) this car for almost nine years.

Answer: have owned

have/has + past participle + for + duration = still true now.

I ___ (find) my passport yet — I should look for it tonight.

Answer: haven't found

haven't/hasn't + past participle + yet = hasn't happened, but probably will soon.

Have you ___ (call) the dentist yet?

Answer: called

Have/Has + subject + past participle ... + yet? = asking if something has happened by now.

My brother ___ (work) abroad since he finished university.

Answer: has worked

since + clause (since he finished university) still needs the present perfect in the main clause.

They ___ (make) a decision yet, so the meeting might run late.

Answer: haven't made

haven't/hasn't + past participle + yet, often followed by a consequence or reason.

Flashcards

for + duration

We've lived in this flat ___ six years.

Back: We've lived in this flat for six years., answer as for

For is used with a period of time — how long something has continued. We say for six years, for a long time, for a few weeks.

She's worked at the hospital for three years. · I haven't seen my cousins for a long time.

for + тривалість: «for six years» — «протягом шести років».

since + point in time

He's worked at this company ___ 2019.

Back: He's worked at this company since 2019., answer as since

Since is used with a specific point when something started — a year, a day, or an event. We say since 2019, since Monday, since last summer.

I've known about the new rules since Monday. · They've lived in Kyiv since last summer.

since + конкретний момент: «since 2019» — «з 2019 року».

since + clause

I've wanted to visit Japan ___ I was a child.

Back: I've wanted to visit Japan since I was a child., answer as since

Since can also introduce a clause with its own subject and verb, showing when something started. The clause after since is usually in the past simple.

We've been friends since we met at university. · She's loved painting since she was at school.

since + речення (since I was a child) — «відтоді як...».

yet in negative sentences

I haven't paid the electricity bill ___.

Back: I haven't paid the electricity bill yet., answer as yet

Yet means 'at any time up to now'. In negative sentences it shows something hasn't happened, but we expect it to happen soon.

She hasn't replied to my email yet. · We haven't booked our flights yet.

yet у заперечних реченнях — «ще не»: «I haven't finished yet».

yet in questions

Has she replied to your message ___?

Back: Has she replied to your message yet?, answer as yet

We use yet in questions to ask if something has happened 'at any time up to now'. Yet usually comes at the end of the question.

Have you finished your homework yet? · Has the bus arrived yet?

yet у питаннях — наприкінці речення: «Have you finished yet?»

already (affirmative) vs yet (negative/question)

I've ___ (already / yet) eaten breakfast, thanks.

Back: I've already eaten breakfast, thanks., answer as already

Already is used in affirmative (positive) sentences to say something has happened, often sooner than expected. Yet is used in negatives and questions instead.

He's already left for work. · We've already seen that film.

already — у стверджувальних реченнях: «I've already eaten» («я вже поїв»).
