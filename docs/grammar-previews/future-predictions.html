🔮 Predictions: will, might & be going to

Unit 5D · 25 questions · 8 cards

Gap Fill

I'm sure we ___ enjoy the concert tonight.

Answer: will

I'm sure + will = a confident prediction.

He's very reliable, so I'm sure he ___ let us down.

Answer: won't

Reliable + sure = won't let you down.

I haven't checked the schedule, so we ___ be free on Friday.

Answer: might

Haven't decided/checked yet = might, not will.

I'm not sure about my plans, so I ___ come to the party.

Answer: might not

Not sure = might not, a softer, less certain negative than won't.

Look at those grey clouds — it ___ rain any minute.

Answer: is going to

Evidence you can see now (clouds, a queue, a mess) = going to, not will.

Experts say screens ___ become much thinner in the next few years.

Answer: will

Experts say/believe/think + will — an opinion-based prediction, not something you can see happening now.

Error Spot

She will probably passes the exam next week.

Correction: pass

After 'will', use the base verb with no -s: 'She will probably pass the exam.'

It's going to raining later.

Correction: rain

'Going to' is followed by the base verb, not the -ing form: 'It's going to rain later.'

She never forgets anything, so I'm sure she will forget your birthday.

Correction: won't

The context (she never forgets) needs a confident NEGATIVE prediction: 'I'm sure she won't forget.'

I haven't decided about the trip, so I will go next week.

Correction: might

Uncertainty ('haven't decided') needs 'might': 'so I might go next week.'

They is going to visit us next weekend.

Correction: are

The plural subject 'they' needs 'are going to', not 'is going to'.

I don't think it might rain later.

Correction: will

'I don't think it will rain' is the natural way to express a negative prediction — not 'I don't think it might rain'.

Multiple Choice

Choose the sentence that shows the speaker feels certain about the future:

Answer: I'm sure the train will arrive on time.

I'm sure + will = certainty.

Choose the sentence that correctly uses 'be going to' for a prediction based on evidence you can see right now:

Answer: Look at the sky – it's going to snow!

Look at the sky — it's going to snow! Evidence you can see now = going to.

A: 'Why do you have your umbrella?' You can see dark clouds outside. Choose the best reply:

Answer: Because I think it's going to rain.

Visible evidence right now (dark clouds) = going to, not will.

Your friend asks if you're coming to the party, but you haven't decided yet. Choose the best reply:

Answer: I might come – I haven't decided yet.

Haven't decided yet = might, never will or won't.

Choose the grammatically correct sentence:

Answer: She definitely won't be late – she's never late.

Probably/definitely + won't — the adverb always comes right before 'won't'.

Choose the sentence that makes a general prediction based on opinion, not something you can see happening right now:

Answer: I think robots will do more jobs in the future.

An opinion about the distant future = will. Visible evidence right now = going to.

Choose the grammatically correct sentence:

Answer: He's probably going to be late again.

He's probably going to be late — the adverb sits between 'be' and 'going to'.

Word Transform

I ___ (call) you as soon as I arrive.

Answer: will call

Promises and confident predictions: will + base verb.

They've lost their three best players, so they ___ (win) the match tonight.

Answer: won't win

Confident 'no' about the future: won't + base verb.

I haven't decided yet, but I ___ (go) to the gym later.

Answer: might go

Haven't decided = might + base verb.

Look at those clouds – it ___ (rain) soon.

Answer: is going to rain

Evidence you can see now: is/are going to + base verb.

I'm not sure about dinner yet – I ___ (cook) tonight.

Answer: might not cook

Not sure + negative idea = might not + base verb.

She's worked so hard all year, so she ___ (pass) her final exams.

Answer: is definitely going to pass

Strongest confidence: definitely + going to, or definitely + will.

Flashcards

will — confident prediction

I'm sure the team ___ (win) tonight.

Back: I'm sure the team will win tonight. Answer as will win

We use will when we feel certain about a prediction, often after phrases like I'm sure or I think.

I'm sure she will get the job. · I think ticket prices will go up next year.

will — впевнений прогноз: I'm sure she will win («я впевнений, що...»).

won't — confident negative prediction

He's very organised, so he ___ (forget) the meeting.

Back: He's very organised, so he won't forget the meeting. Answer as won't forget

Won't (will not) makes a confident negative prediction — we are sure something will not happen.

Don't worry, we won't be late. · I'm sure she won't mind waiting a few minutes.

won't — впевнений прогноз «не»: I'm sure he won't be late.

might — uncertain prediction

We haven't decided yet, so we ___ (go) camping this summer.

Back: We haven't decided yet, so we might go camping this summer. Answer as might go

Might shows we are not sure about a prediction — it's a possibility, not a certainty.

They might win the game, but they might not. · I might take the early train tomorrow.

might — можливість, коли не впевнені: We might go («можливо, підемо»).

might not — uncertain negative prediction

I'm feeling ill, so I ___ (come) to work tomorrow.

Back: I'm feeling ill, so I might not come to work tomorrow. Answer as might not come

Might not shows it's possible something won't happen, but we can't be certain either way.

She might not finish the report today. · We might not have time to visit everyone.

might not — можливо, що ні: I might not come («можливо, я не прийду»).

be going to — evidence-based prediction

Look at those dark clouds — it ___ (will / going to) rain soon.

Back: Look at those dark clouds — it is going to rain soon. Answer as is going to

We use be going to when we can see evidence right now that something will happen.

Look out, that glass is going to fall off the table! · She's exhausted — she's going to sleep all weekend.

be going to — прогноз на основі доказів зараз: it's going to rain!

will + probably/definitely — adverb after will

She's a great student, so she ___ (pass) all her exams.

Back: She's a great student, so she will probably pass all her exams. Answer as will probably pass

Probably and definitely usually come straight after will, not before it: she'll probably pass, not she probably will pass.

He'll definitely be late — he always is. · They'll probably arrive before lunch.

probably/definitely стоять одразу після will: she'll probably pass.

definitely/probably + won't — before won't

He's missed the bus three times this week, so he ___ (be) on time today.

Back: He's missed the bus three times this week, so he definitely won't be on time today. Answer as definitely won't be

With won't, probably and definitely come before it, not after: she probably won't call, not she won't probably call.

I definitely won't be able to make the meeting. · She probably won't call again tonight.

З won't ці слова йдуть ПЕРЕД ним: she probably won't call.

probably/definitely with be going to

She's running really late, so she ___ (miss) the bus.

Back: She's running really late, so she is probably going to miss the bus. Answer as is probably going to miss

We can also use probably and definitely with be going to, placed between be and going to.

He's definitely not going to meet us there. · We're probably going to be late for the film.

З be going to ці слова стоять між be і going to: she's probably going to miss it.
