🧮 Quantifiers

Lesson 5B — 25 questions · 8 cards

Gap Fill

Would you like ___ tea?

Answer: some

Offers and requests use 'some': Would you like some water? Could I have some help?

Do you have ___ brothers or sisters?

Answer: any

Ordinary questions use 'any': Do you have any pets? Is there any milk?

There's ___ milk left in the fridge — we need to buy more.

Answer: no

no + positive verb = not any: There's no milk = There isn't any milk.

How ___ time do we have before the train leaves?

Answer: much

How much + uncountable: how much time, how much money, how much water.

There aren't ___ seats left — only five or six.

Answer: many

many + countable plural: many seats, many people, many days.

I can't concentrate — there are ___ distractions in this office.

Answer: too many

too many + countable (problem): too many distractions, too many emails, too many mistakes.

Error Spot

I don't have no time to explain this now.

Correction: any

After a negative verb, use 'any': 'I don't have any time to explain this now.'

There are no information about the flight.

Correction: is

Uncountable nouns take a singular verb: 'There is no information about the flight.'

I don't have much friends in this city.

Correction: many

'Friends' is countable, so the correct quantifier is 'many': 'I don't have many friends in this city.'

I don't have lot of money saved for the trip.

Correction: a lot

Add 'a' before 'lot': 'I don't have a lot of money saved for the trip.'

There are too much people at this party.

Correction: many

'People' is countable, so use 'too many': 'There are too many people at this party.'

I need a little advices before the interview.

Correction: advice

'Advice' has no plural form: 'I need a little advice before the interview.'

Multiple Choice

Which sentence is a polite offer?

Answer: Would you like some coffee?

Offers use 'some': Would you like some coffee?

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: I don't have much time today.

much + uncountable: much time, much money, much water.

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: How many apples do we need?

How many + countable plural: how many apples, how many people.

Which sentence describes a problem correctly?

Answer: There's too much traffic this morning.

too much + uncountable (problem): too much traffic, too much noise, too much work.

Which sentence uses 'enough' correctly?

Answer: We don't have enough chairs for everyone.

enough + noun = the right amount: enough chairs, enough time, enough money.

Which sentence uses 'a little' correctly?

Answer: I need a little help with this form.

a little + uncountable: a little help, a little time. a few + countable plural: a few people.

Which sentence correctly talks about something in general?

Answer: Fashion isn't very important to me.

General statements: no article, no quantifier — Fashion isn't important to me. People are interesting.

Word Transform

Could you give me ___ (some / any) advice about buying a car?

Answer: some

Requests use 'some': Could I have some water? Could you give me some advice?

There's ___ (no / not) parking near the office, so we should take the bus.

Answer: no

no + positive verb = not any: There's no parking = There isn't any parking.

We didn't make ___ (much / many) mistakes in the test.

Answer: many

many + countable plural: many mistakes, many friends, many days.

She's stressed because she has ___ (too much / too many) work this month.

Answer: too much

too much + uncountable: too much work, too much stress, too much noise.

Can I have ___ (a bit of / a little) sugar in my coffee, please?

Answer: a little

a little / a bit of + uncountable: a little sugar, a bit of time.

I only had $20, so I didn't have ___ (enough / much) money to buy the tickets.

Answer: enough

enough + noun = the right amount for a purpose: enough money, enough time, enough space.

Flashcards

some in positive sentences, offers and requests

There's ___ cake left in the kitchen — help yourself! as ___

Back: There's some cake left in the kitchen — help yourself!, answer as some

We use 'some' in positive sentences when we don't say the exact amount. It also sounds natural in offers and requests, even though they're questions.

I bought some bread and some cheese at the shop. · Could I have some water, please?

some = «трохи, якась кількість» (не точна): There's some cake — «Є трохи торта». Також у пропозиціях: Would you like some tea? — «Хочеш чаю?»

any in questions and negatives

I don't have ___ cash on me right now. as ___

Back: I don't have any cash on me right now., answer as any

We use 'any' in negative sentences to say something doesn't exist, and in most questions to ask if it exists.

Is there any sugar in the cupboard? · We didn't see any dolphins on the boat trip.

any вживаємо в питаннях і заперечних реченнях: Do you have any cash? — «У тебе є готівка?» I don't have any cash — «У мене немає готівки».

no = not any (with a positive verb)

There's ___ sugar in the jar — it's completely empty. as ___

Back: There's no sugar in the jar — it's completely empty., answer as no

'No' + noun means the same as 'not any', but it goes with a positive verb, not a negative one.

There's no bread left — we ate it all. · He has no free time this week.

no = «ніякий, зовсім немає», але дієслово ставимо у стверджувальній формі: There's no sugar = There isn't any sugar.

a lot of / lots of — a large amount

She's got ___ (a lot of / lots of) experience in this job. as ___

Back: She's got a lot of experience in this job., answer as a lot of

'A lot of' and 'lots of' both mean a large amount. They work with countable and uncountable nouns, in positive, negative and question sentences.

We spent a lot of money on the trip. · There are lots of good restaurants in this area.

a lot of / lots of = «багато» (велика кількість), підходять і до злічуваних, і до незлічуваних іменників: a lot of money, a lot of people.

much with uncountable nouns, many with countable nouns

How ___ (much / many) luggage have you got? as ___

Back: How much luggage have you got?, answer as much

'Much' goes with uncountable nouns (like 'luggage'), and 'many' goes with countable plural nouns (like 'bags'). Both are common in questions and negatives.

I haven't got much money left. · There aren't many buses after 10 p.m.

much — з незлічуваними (much luggage), many — зі злічуваними у множині (many bags). Частіше в питаннях і запереченнях.

too much / too many — more than necessary (a problem)

There was ___ (too much / too many) noise at the concert — I couldn't hear anything. as ___

Back: There was too much noise at the concert — I couldn't hear anything., answer as too much

'Too much' (uncountable) and 'too many' (countable) show that an amount is a problem — more than is good or necessary.

I ate too much cake and now I feel sick. · There were too many people in the small room.

too much (незлічувані) / too many (злічувані) = «забагато», коли кількість — проблема: too much noise, too many people.

enough — the right amount, before the noun

We haven't got ___ chairs for all the guests. as ___

Back: We haven't got enough chairs for all the guests., answer as enough

'Enough' means the right or necessary amount. Before a noun, it goes directly in front of it: 'enough chairs'.

Have we got enough food for the party? · There isn't enough time to finish today.

enough = «достатньо», ставимо прямо перед іменником: enough chairs — «достатньо стільців».

a bit of / a little — a small amount

I only need ___ (a bit of / a little) help with my homework. as ___

Back: I only need a little help with my homework., answer as a little

'A bit of' and 'a little' both mean a small amount. They are used with uncountable nouns, like 'help', 'time' or 'money'.

Could you give me a little more time? · There's a bit of milk left in the bottle.

a bit of / a little = «трохи» (невелика кількість), тільки з незлічуваними іменниками: a little time, a bit of milk.
