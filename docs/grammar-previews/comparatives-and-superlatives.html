⚖️ Comparatives and Superlatives

Lesson 6B · 25 questions · 8 cards

Gap Fill

My brother is ___ than me.

Answer: taller

One-syllable adjectives: add -er for the comparative, -est for the superlative.

This is ___ restaurant in the city.

Answer: the most expensive

Long adjectives (2+ syllables, not ending in -y): the most/the least + adjective.

The soup tastes ___ than yesterday.

Answer: better

good→better→the best — memorise this one, it has no regular pattern.

My new job isn't ___ my last one.

Answer: as stressful as

as + adjective + as = equal. not as + adjective + as = less.

She can run ___ than her brother.

Answer: faster

Short adverbs (fast, hard, late, early) take -er/-est, just like short adjectives.

He did ___ in the test than he expected.

Answer: worse

well→better, badly→worse — both adjectives and adverbs share these irregular forms.

Error Spot

My new office is biger than my old one.

Correction: bigger

Big ends in a single vowel + consonant, so the final letter doubles before -er: bigger, not biger.

This app is importanter than the last version.

Correction: more important

Long adjectives never take -er — use 'more important', never importanter.

The new office is farer from the station than the old one.

Correction: further

Far is irregular: far → further/farther, never farer.

This laptop isn't as fast than my old one.

Correction: as

'As...as' needs 'as' on both sides: isn't as fast as, not isn't as fast than.

He drives more carefuller than his brother.

Correction: carefully

-ly adverbs never take -er — 'more' already does that job: more carefully.

She can speak French weller than I can.

Correction: better

Well is irregular: well → better, never weller.

Multiple Choice

Choose the sentence with the correct comparative form:

Answer: This bag is heavier than that one.

happy→happier, easy→easier, heavy→heavier — y becomes i before -er/-est.

Choose the sentence with the correct superlative form:

Answer: It's the most comfortable sofa in the shop.

Long adjectives (2+ syllables, not ending in -y): the most/the least + adjective.

Choose the sentence with the correct superlative form:

Answer: That was the worst meal I've ever had.

good→best, bad→worst, far→furthest — learn these three irregular superlatives together.

This coat isn't as expensive as the other one.

Answer: The coat costs less than the other one.

not as expensive as = cheaper. not as good as = worse. as...as (no 'not') = equal.

Choose the sentence with the correct comparative adverb:

Answer: He speaks more fluently than his sister.

Most -ly adverbs use more/most. Only a few short adverbs (fast, hard) add -er/-est.

Choose the sentence where the superlative is used correctly:

Answer: That's her best painting.

the best friend OR her best friend — never 'the her best friend'.

Choose the sentence with the correct comparative form:

Answer: She ran further than anyone else in the race.

Further and farther are both correct — British English often prefers 'further'.

Word Transform

This laptop is ___ (light) than my old one.

Answer: lighter

One-syllable adjectives: add -er. light→lighter, fast→faster, small→smaller.

She is the ___ (talented) person I've ever met.

Answer: most talented

Three-syllable (or longer) adjectives always use most/the most, never -est.

The traffic today is ___ (bad) than yesterday.

Answer: worse

bad→worse→the worst — just like good→better→the best.

My brother isn't as ___ (confident) as me.

Answer: confident

as...as and not as...as always use the plain adjective: as tall as, not as taller as.

He checked the contract ___ (careful) than before.

Answer: more carefully

Turn the adjective into the -ly adverb first, then add more: careful → carefully → more carefully.

Of all my colleagues, she works ___ (hard).

Answer: the hardest

Short adverbs like hard, fast and late form the superlative the same way as short adjectives: + the -est.

Flashcards

short adjectives: -er / -est

My new phone is ___ (fast) than my old one.

Back: My new phone is faster than my old one. Answer as faster

For one-syllable adjectives, add -er for the comparative and -est for the superlative: fast → faster → the fastest.

This is the fastest way to get to the airport. · Her car is cheaper than mine, but mine is more reliable.

короткі прикметники: +er / the ...est: fast → faster → the fastest.

-y adjectives: y → i + -er/-est

The exam was ___ (easy) than I expected.

Back: The exam was easier than I expected. Answer as easier

Two-syllable adjectives ending in -y change y to i before adding -er or -est: easy → easier → the easiest.

She's the happiest person I know. · This recipe is easier than the one we tried last week.

прикметники на -y: y→i: easy → easier → the easiest.

CVC adjectives: double the final consonant

This flat is ___ (big) than our last one.

Back: This flat is bigger than our last one. Answer as bigger

Short adjectives ending in one vowel + one consonant double the final consonant before -er/-est: big → bigger → the biggest.

Today is hotter than yesterday. · That's the biggest mistake I've ever made at work.

один голосний + один приголосний: подвоюємо приголосну: big → bigger → the biggest.

longer adjectives: more / most + adjective

I think working from home is ___ (convenient) than commuting every day.

Back: I think working from home is more convenient than commuting every day. Answer as more convenient

Adjectives with two or more syllables (except those ending in -y) use more/the most instead of -er/-est: convenient → more convenient → the most convenient.

This is the most expensive restaurant in town. · Her new job is more stressful than her last one.

довгі прикметники: more / the most + adjective: expensive → more expensive.

irregular adjectives: good, bad, far

The weather today is much ___ (bad) than it was yesterday.

Back: The weather today is much worse than it was yesterday. Answer as worse

Good, bad and far have irregular comparative and superlative forms: good → better → the best; bad → worse → the worst; far → further/farther → the furthest/farthest.

This is the best coffee I've ever had. · We had to walk further than we planned.

неправильні форми: good→better→best, bad→worse→worst, far→further→furthest.

(not) as + adjective + as

This hotel isn't as ___ (expensive) as the one we stayed in last year.

Back: This hotel isn't as expensive as the one we stayed in last year. Answer as expensive

We use (not) as + adjective + as to say two things are equal, or not equal, in some quality. 'Not as...as' usually means less: not as expensive as = cheaper than.

He isn't as tall as his brother. · This test was just as difficult as the last one.

(not) as + прикметник + as — порівняння: not as expensive as = дешевше.

comparative and superlative adverbs

She speaks English ___ (fluent) than she did a year ago.

Back: She speaks English more fluently than she did a year ago. Answer as more fluently

Adverbs form comparatives and superlatives the same way as adjectives: short adverbs add -er/-est (fast→faster), most adverbs (especially -ly ones) use more/most (fluently→more fluently). Well and badly are irregular: well→better, badly→worse.

He explained it more clearly than before. · Of all the students, she works the hardest.

прислівники: fast→faster; fluently→more fluently; well→better, badly→worse (виняток).

superlatives: the + -est/most, often with present perfect + ever

This is the ___ (interesting) book I've ever read.

Back: This is the most interesting book I've ever read. Answer as most interesting

We normally use 'the' before a superlative, though a possessive adjective is also possible ('her best friend'). Superlatives are often followed by the present perfect + ever to talk about someone's whole experience so far.

That's the funniest film I've ever seen. · He's the most talented musician in the band.

найвищий ступінь часто з Present Perfect + ever: the most interesting book I've ever read.
