🕰️ used to

Unit 6A — 25 questions · 8 cards

Gap Fill

As a child, I ___ collect toy cars.

Answer: used to

used to + base verb = a habit in the past that has stopped.

She ___ live in a small village before she moved to the city.

Answer: used to

used to can describe a past state too, not just a repeated action: used to live, used to have, used to be.

I ___ to like vegetables when I was young, but now I love them.

Answer: didn't use

Negative: didn't use to (not didn't used to) — the 'd' moves to 'did'.

___ you use to play an instrument when you were at school?

Answer: Did

Question: Did you use to...? The 'd' belongs to 'did', never to 'use'.

Last year, our class ___ to a museum for a school trip.

Answer: went

A single event or a stated number of times/years → past simple, never used to.

A: Do you still play tennis every day? B: No, I ___, but I don't anymore.

Answer: used to

Short answers can stop at 'used to' — the activity is understood from the question.

Error Spot

He use to draw every day at school.

Correction: used

Affirmative 'used to' always has the 'd': 'He used to draw every day at school.'

She didn't used to eat meat.

Correction: use

After 'didn't', drop the 'd': 'She didn't use to eat meat.'

Did you used to live in Paris?

Correction: use

After 'did', drop the 'd': 'Did you use to live in Paris?'

She used to goes to the gym every day.

Correction: go

After 'used to', use the base verb: 'She used to go to the gym every day.'

He used to playing football every weekend.

Correction: play

After 'used to', use the base verb, not -ing: 'He used to play football every weekend.'

They used to lived in Berlin when they were young.

Correction: live

After 'used to', use the base verb, not the past tense: 'They used to live in Berlin when they were young.'

Multiple Choice

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: He used to play the guitar, but he gave it up years ago.

used to + infinitive = true in the past, not true now.

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: I didn't use to enjoy reading, but now I read every night.

didn't use to — remember the missing 'd'.

Which sentence describes a past state correctly?

Answer: She used to be very shy, but she's much more confident now.

used to + be/have/live = a state that is no longer true.

Which sentence has an error?

Answer: Last March I used to visit some friends in Mexico City.

A single event, a number, or a length of time → never 'used to'.

Which question is grammatically correct?

Answer: Did you use to collect anything when you were a child?

Did + subject + use to + base verb — never 'Did ... used to'.

A: Do you still play tennis? B: ...

Answer: No, I used to, but I don't anymore.

A: Do you still skateboard? B: No, I used to. — the activity doesn't need repeating.

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: I never used to see my cousins, except in the summer holidays.

never used to (with 'd') is a common spoken alternative to didn't use to (without 'd').

Word Transform

We ___ (live) close to the train station, but we moved away last year.

Answer: used to live

used to + base verb, always the same form for every subject.

Elena ___ (drink) a lot of coffee, but she doesn't anymore.

Answer: used to drink

used to + base verb works the same for he/she/it as for I/you/we/they.

I ___ (like) my job, but I love it now.

Answer: didn't use to like

Negative: didn't use to + base verb.

Did you ___ (have) art lessons when you were young?

Answer: use to have

Question: Did + subject + use to + base verb (no 'd' on 'use').

My parents ___ (watch) a lot of television, but they don't now.

Answer: used to watch

used to + base verb for a habit that no longer continues.

It never ___ (be) so hot in the summers here, but things have changed.

Answer: used to be

never used to + base verb — 'used' keeps its 'd' after 'never'.

Flashcards

used to + base verb — past habit

As a child, Marco ___ stamps every weekend.

Back: As a child, Marco used to collect stamps every weekend. Answer as used to collect

'Used to' + the base form of the verb describes something that happened regularly in the past but doesn't happen now. The form never changes, whoever is doing it.

We used to walk to school together every morning. · My grandmother used to tell us stories before bed.

used to + дієслово — звичка в минулому, якої вже немає: used to collect («колись збирав»).

used to + base verb — past state

Before she moved abroad, Anna ___ in a tiny flat.

Back: Before she moved abroad, Anna used to live in a tiny flat. Answer as used to live

'Used to' also describes a state that lasted for a time in the past — where someone lived, what they owned, how they felt — and is no longer true.

He used to be very quiet at school. · They used to have a dog, but it died two years ago.

used to описує і стан у минулому, не лише дію: used to live, used to be.

negative: didn't use to (no 'd')

I ___ spicy food, but now it's my favourite.

Back: I didn't use to enjoy spicy food, but now it's my favourite. Answer as didn't use to enjoy

The negative is 'didn't use to' + base verb. Because 'didn't' already carries the past tense, 'use' drops its 'd'.

She didn't use to go out much, but she's much more social now. · We didn't use to travel abroad very often.

заперечення: didn't use to (без «d» на use!) — didn't used to є помилкою.

question: Did ... use to? (no 'd')

Did you ___ a musical instrument as a child?

Back: Did you use to play a musical instrument as a child? Answer as use to play

Questions are formed with 'did' + subject + 'use to' + base verb. Again, 'use' has no 'd' once 'did' is carrying the past tense.

Did you use to live near the sea? · Did they use to visit you every summer?

питання: Did + підмет + use to (без «d»): Did you use to play...?

used to or past simple?

She ___ a photographer before she became a teacher.

Back: She used to be a photographer before she became a teacher. Answer as used to be

'Used to' and the past simple can both describe a repeated past habit or lasting state — both are correct and mean almost the same thing.

I used to play tennis every weekend. · I played tennis every weekend.

used to і Past Simple часто взаємозамінні для звички/стану в минулому: обидва варіанти правильні.

one-off events: past simple only

Last summer, my family ___ to a new city.

Back: Last summer, my family moved to a new city. Answer as moved

'Used to' cannot describe something that happened only once, a stated number of times, or for a stated length of time — only the past simple works for those.

We went to Portugal twice last year. · I studied French for two years at school.

used to не можна для разової події, точної кількості разів чи періоду — лише Past Simple: moved, went, studied.

never used to (spoken alternative)

My brother ___ classical music, but he does now.

Back: My brother never used to like classical music, but he does now. Answer as never used to like

In spoken English, 'never used to' is often preferred to 'didn't use to'. With 'never' instead of 'didn't', 'used' keeps its 'd'.

I never used to eat breakfast, but I do every day now. · She never used to drive, but she passed her test last year.

в розмовній мові часто never used to (з «d», бо never — не допоміжне дієслово) замість didn't use to.

short answers: verb often dropped

A: Do you still skateboard? B: No, I ___, but I don't anymore.

Back: A: Do you still skateboard? B: No, I used to, but I don't anymore. Answer as used to

In short answers, the verb after 'used to' is often left out completely, because it's already clear from the question.

A: Do you still play the piano? B: I used to, but I stopped years ago. · A: Did you use to collect anything? B: Yes, I used to.

у коротких відповідях дієслово після used to часто опускають: I used to (без повторення дії).
