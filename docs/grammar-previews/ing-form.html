➿ The -ing Form

Lesson 8D — 25 questions · 8 cards

Gap Fill

I suggest ___ a few days before the exam to relax.

Answer: taking

suggest, recommend, keep, practise = always + -ing, never + to.

Stop interrupting! You keep ___ the same thing.

Answer: saying

keep + -ing = do something again and again.

The dentist recommends ___ your teeth twice a day.

Answer: brushing

recommend + -ing — never recommend + to.

I practise ___ English with my colleagues every lunchtime.

Answer: speaking

practise + -ing, like suggest and recommend.

He can't stand ___ in long queues at the supermarket.

Answer: waiting

can't stand, wouldn't mind, look forward to = expressions + -ing.

We look forward to ___ you again soon.

Answer: seeing

look forward TO + -ing — 'to' here is a preposition, not an infinitive marker.

Error Spot

She suggested go to the new Italian restaurant.

Correction: going

After 'suggest', use the -ing form: 'suggested going', not the base form 'go'.

You keep make the same mistake in your essays.

Correction: making

After 'keep', use the -ing form: 'keep making', not the base form 'make'.

Can you imagine live without a phone for a week?

Correction: living

After 'imagine', use the -ing form: 'imagine living', not the base form 'live'.

He practises play the piano every evening.

Correction: playing

After 'practise', use the -ing form: 'practises playing', not the base form 'play'.

The guide recommended visit the old town at sunset.

Correction: visiting

After 'recommend', use the -ing form: 'recommended visiting', not the base form 'visit'.

I can't stand wait for buses in the rain.

Correction: waiting

After 'can't stand', use the -ing form: 'can't stand waiting', not the base form 'wait'.

Multiple Choice

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: I hate being late for meetings.

like, hate, begin, continue — both -ing and to + infinitive work, with almost no change in meaning.

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: It began raining just as we left the house.

begin + -ing (or to + infinitive) — but never mix the two: began to raining is wrong.

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: They delayed announcing the winner until Friday.

delay + -ing — never delay + to or the base form.

Which sentence describes a MEMORY of something that already happened, not a future duty?

Answer: I remember meeting her at the conference last year.

remember doing = a memory of the past. remember to do = don't forget (future).

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: I'm looking forward to seeing you this weekend.

look forward TO + -ing — 'to' is a preposition here, not an infinitive marker.

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: I wouldn't mind trying that new restaurant.

wouldn't mind + -ing — never wouldn't mind + to.

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: She suggested studying together before the exam.

Never say 'suggest someone to do' — say 'suggest doing' or 'suggest that someone do'.

Word Transform

My little brother likes ___ (build) towers out of blocks.

Answer: building

like, hate, begin + -ing or to infinitive — both are fine.

The baby began ___ (crawl) at eight months old.

Answer: crawling

begin + -ing (or to + infinitive) — little change in meaning.

The airline delayed ___ (announce) the departure gate.

Answer: announcing

delay + -ing — never delay + to.

My dad hates ___ (queue) at the post office.

Answer: queuing

hate + -ing (or to + infinitive) — little change in meaning.

I'll always remember ___ (watch) my first football match with my grandfather.

Answer: watching

remember doing = a memory of the past. remember to do = don't forget (future).

After the long hike, I wouldn't mind ___ (rest) for a while.

Answer: resting

wouldn't mind + -ing — never wouldn't mind + to.

Flashcards

begin, delay + -ing

After the interval, the orchestra began ___ the second half of the concert.

Back: After the interval, the orchestra began playing the second half of the concert., answer as playing

Begin and delay can be followed by the -ing form (as well as to + infinitive, with little difference in meaning).

The company delayed launching the new product until spring. · He began exercising every morning after New Year.

begin, delay + -ing (або to): began playing ≈ began to play.

hate, like + -ing (or to)

My sister hates ___ in long queues at the airport.

Back: My sister hates waiting in long queues at the airport., answer as waiting

After hate and like, you can use the -ing form or to + infinitive with almost no change in meaning: hate waiting = hate to wait.

I like reading on rainy afternoons. · We hate arriving late to parties.

hate/like + -ing або to: майже без різниці у значенні.

imagine + -ing

I can't imagine ___ in a country where I don't speak the language.

Back: I can't imagine living in a country where I don't speak the language., answer as living

Imagine is always followed by the -ing form — we picture the action happening.

Can you imagine working from a beach every day? · I imagine moving to a new city would be exciting.

imagine + -ing: «уявити, як...» → imagine living, не imagine to live.

keep + -ing

The printer keeps ___ in the middle of the page.

Back: The printer keeps stopping in the middle of the page., answer as stopping

Keep + -ing describes an action that happens again and again, often something annoying.

My neighbour's dog keeps barking at night. · Keep trying — you'll get it eventually!

keep + -ing: дія, що повторюється: keeps stopping «постійно зупиняється».

practise + -ing

Every Saturday, she practises ___ in the empty car park.

Back: Every Saturday, she practises driving in the empty car park., answer as driving

Practise is always followed by the -ing form, not to + infinitive.

He practises answering interview questions with his sister. · We should practise speaking English more often.

practise + -ing (не to): practises driving, не practises to drive.

recommend, suggest + -ing

The waiter recommended ___ the fish special.

Back: The waiter recommended trying the fish special., answer as trying

Recommend and suggest are followed by the -ing form, never by to + infinitive.

I suggest booking the tickets early. · The doctor recommends drinking more water.

recommend, suggest + -ing: suggest booking, не suggest to book.

remember + -ing (a past memory)

I still remember ___ my bike for the first time.

Back: I still remember riding my bike for the first time., answer as riding

Remember + -ing talks about a memory of something that already happened. Compare: remember to do = don't forget to do it in the future.

Do you remember meeting at that wedding years ago? · She remembers feeling nervous on her first day at work.

remember + -ing — спогад про минуле, на відміну від remember to do (не забути).

can't stand, look forward to, wouldn't mind + -ing

I wouldn't mind ___ a bit longer if that's okay.

Back: I wouldn't mind staying a bit longer if that's okay., answer as staying

Can't stand, look forward to and wouldn't mind are all followed by the -ing form.

I can't stand waiting in traffic every morning. · We're looking forward to visiting you next month.

can't stand, look forward to, wouldn't mind + -ing: look forward to seeing, не to see.
