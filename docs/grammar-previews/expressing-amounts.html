🧳 Quantifiers: Large and Small Amounts

Unit 7B — 25 questions · 8 cards

Gap Fill

I don't need much — just give me ___ information about the hotel before I book it.

Answer: a bit of

a bit of / a little + uncountable: a bit of information, a little time, a little money.

We checked the fridge, but there was ___ food left — just one egg.

Answer: hardly any

hardly any = almost none: hardly any food, hardly any people.

Do you spend ___ time travelling for work?

Answer: a lot of

a lot of works everywhere: positive, negative, questions. plenty of is mostly for positive statements.

This box isn't light ___ to carry up the stairs on my own.

Answer: enough

adjective + enough: light enough, old enough, warm enough.

There's ___ traffic on the motorway this morning, so we'll probably be late.

Answer: too much

too much + uncountable (problem): too much traffic, too much noise.

Don't worry about snacks for the trip — we've already got ___ biscuits and fruit for everyone.

Answer: plenty of

plenty of = more than enough, no problem: plenty of biscuits, plenty of time.

Error Spot

There's a few milk left in the fridge, so we need to buy more.

Correction: little

'Milk' is uncountable, so use 'a little': 'There's a little milk left in the fridge.'

I'd like a little biscuits with my tea, please.

Correction: few

'Biscuits' is countable, so use 'a few': 'I'd like a few biscuits with my tea.'

There are hardly some people in the waiting room today.

Correction: any

'Hardly' always pairs with 'any': 'There are hardly any people in the waiting room today.'

There are too much tourists in this city every summer.

Correction: many

'Tourists' is countable, so use 'too many': 'There are too many tourists in this city every summer.'

She has much confidence in her new job.

Correction: a lot of

Positive statements use 'a lot of', not 'much': 'She has a lot of confidence in her new job.'

I haven't got hardly any money this month.

Correction: have

Don't combine 'hardly any' with a negative verb: 'I have got hardly any money this month.'

Multiple Choice

Choose the sentence that correctly uses 'plenty of'.

Answer: We have plenty of time before the film starts.

plenty of + noun: plenty of time, plenty of money — never 'a plenty of' or 'plenty' alone before a noun.

Choose the sentence that correctly uses 'a few' with a countable noun.

Answer: I packed a few shirts for the trip.

a few + countable plural: a few shirts, a few days, a few people.

Choose the sentence that describes a very small amount, almost none.

Answer: There's hardly any coffee left in the pot.

hardly any = almost none: hardly any coffee, hardly any time.

Choose the sentence where 'too' shows there's a problem, not just a high degree.

Answer: This soup is too spicy for me to eat.

too + adjective = a problem. very + adjective = just a high degree, no problem.

Choose the sentence that puts 'enough' in the correct position with an adjective.

Answer: Is the water warm enough for a swim?

adjective + enough: warm enough, old enough, fast enough.

Choose the sentence that correctly expresses a large amount in a positive statement.

Answer: We spent a lot of money on the holiday.

Positive statements: a lot of / lots of money. Negatives and questions: much money.

Choose the question that correctly asks about a large, unspecified quantity.

Answer: Do you spend a lot of time online?

a lot of = works in every sentence type. plenty of = mainly positive statements.

Word Transform

There isn't ___ sugar left for the cake — we need to buy more.

Answer: much

much + uncountable, mainly in negatives and questions: isn't much sugar, how much sugar?

I've only got ___ dollars, so I can't buy a new phone.

Answer: a few

a few + countable plural: a few dollars, a few days, a few friends.

We're really lucky — we've got ___ fresh fruit from the garden this year.

Answer: plenty of

plenty of = a large amount, no problem. too much = a large amount, a problem.

There's ___ milk in the fridge — just a drop, really.

Answer: hardly any

hardly any = almost none: hardly any milk, hardly any time.

This jacket isn't warm ___ for the winter here.

Answer: enough

adjective + enough: warm enough, big enough, fast enough.

The train was ___ crowded for me to find a seat.

Answer: too

too + adjective + a result clause = a problem: too crowded to find a seat.

Flashcards

plenty of — a large amount (not a problem)

We've got ___ (plenty of / a lot of) time before the flight, so let's relax.

Back: We've got plenty of time before the flight, so let's relax. Answer as plenty of

'Plenty of' means a large amount — enough and more. It's mostly used in positive statements, with countable or uncountable nouns.

There's plenty of parking outside the cinema. · She has plenty of friends in this city.

plenty of ≈ «достатньо й навіть більше» (переважно у стверджувальних реченнях).

a few — small number (countable nouns only)

I've invited ___ neighbours round for coffee tomorrow.

Back: I've invited a few neighbours round for coffee tomorrow. Answer as a few

'A few' means a small number. It only goes with countable nouns, like 'neighbours' or 'days'.

We stayed in Rome for a few days. · I only need a few more minutes to finish this.

a few = «кілька» — тільки зі злічуваними іменниками: a few days «кілька днів».

a bit of / a little — small amount (uncountable nouns only)

Could I have ___ (a bit of / a little) more time to think about it?

Back: Could I have a little more time to think about it? Answer as a little

'A bit of' and 'a little' both mean a small amount. They go with uncountable nouns, like 'time' or 'milk', never with plural countable nouns.

There's a bit of cheese left in the fridge. · I need a little help moving this sofa.

a bit of / a little = «трохи» — тільки з незлічуваними: a little time «трохи часу».

hardly any — a very small amount, almost none

I looked in my wallet, but there was ___ cash inside — just one coin.

Back: I looked in my wallet, but there was hardly any cash inside — just one coin. Answer as hardly any

'Hardly any' means a very small amount — almost none. It works with both countable and uncountable nouns.

There were hardly any seats left on the bus. · We've got hardly any sugar, so the tea will be weak.

hardly any = «майже нічого, дуже мало»: hardly any money «майже немає грошей».

too much / too many — more than is wanted or needed

He put ___ salt in the soup, so now it tastes awful.

Back: He put too much salt in the soup, so now it tastes awful. Answer as too much

'Too much' (uncountable) and 'too many' (countable) show that an amount is a problem — more than we want or need.

There were too many people at the meeting to fit in the room. · I ate too much cake and now I feel sick.

too much (незлічувані) / too many (злічувані) = «забагато» — коли кількість стає проблемою.

enough — the right amount (before a noun or after an adjective)

This shirt isn't big ___ for my brother — he needs a bigger size.

Back: This shirt isn't big enough for my brother — he needs a bigger size. Answer as enough

'Enough' means the right amount. Before a noun it goes in front: 'enough time'. After an adjective, it goes straight after: 'big enough'.

Is there enough milk for breakfast? · She's not old enough to drive yet.

enough після прикметника: big enough «достатньо великий»; перед іменником: enough time «достатньо часу».

too + adjective — a problem, not the same as 'very'

The hotel room was ___ small for all four of us to sleep in.

Back: The hotel room was too small for all four of us to sleep in. Answer as too

'Too' + adjective means more than we want or need — there's a problem. It's different from 'very', which just shows a high degree with no problem.

This coffee is too hot to drink right now. · The music was too loud, so we couldn't talk.

too + прикметник = «занадто», є проблема. Не плутати з very («дуже», без проблеми).

much — not used in positive statements

How ___ rain did you get last winter?

Back: How much rain did you get last winter? Answer as much

'Much' goes with uncountable nouns in questions and negative sentences: 'How much rain...?', 'I don't have much time.' In positive statements, use 'a lot of' or 'lots of' instead.

I don't have much energy today. · We don't usually get much snow here.

much — тільки в питаннях/запереченнях; у стверджувальних реченнях кажемо a lot of.
