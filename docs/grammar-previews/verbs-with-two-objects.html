🎁 Verbs with Two Objects

Lesson 5D — 25 questions · 6 cards

Gap Fill

Could you lend ___ some money until Friday?

Answer: me

verb + person + thing, no preposition: lend me money, give me a book.

She sent the photos ___ her best friend yesterday.

Answer: to

send/give/show/pass/teach/write/offer + thing + to + person.

Could you get a chair ___ grandma? She needs somewhere to sit.

Answer: for

buy/get/leave/make + thing + for + person.

He asked ___ a difficult question in the interview.

Answer: me

ask/tell + person + thing — never add to or for.

I'll leave the tickets ___ you at the reception desk.

Answer: for

buy/get/leave/make + thing + for + person.

I told ___ the truth, even though it was difficult.

Answer: her

tell + person + thing — not 'tell the truth to someone' in this pattern.

Error Spot

He sent the invitation for his new neighbours.

Correction: to

'Send' uses 'to' before the person: 'He sent the invitation to his new neighbours.'

He bought a present to his wife.

Correction: for

'Buy' uses 'for' before the person: 'He bought a present for his wife.'

Our teacher taught grammar for us every Friday.

Correction: to

'Teach' uses 'to' before the person: 'Our teacher taught grammar to us every Friday.'

She asked I many questions about the trip.

Correction: me

Use the object pronoun: 'She asked me many questions about the trip.'

I left a message to my sister this morning.

Correction: for

'Leave' uses 'for' before the person: 'I left a message for my sister this morning.'

He made a cake to his mother last weekend.

Correction: for

'Make' uses 'for' before the person: 'He made a cake for his mother last weekend.'

Word Transform

Could you pass ___ (I/me) the menu, please?

Answer: me

verb + person: use object pronouns (me, him, us), never subject pronouns (I, he, we).

She showed ___ (we/us) her new flat on Saturday.

Answer: us

verb + person: object pronoun, not subject pronoun.

Answer: to

Answer: for

buy/get/leave/make + thing + for + person.

Answer: to

Flashcards

verb + person + thing (no preposition)

My brother lent ___ (I) his car for the weekend.

Back: My brother lent me his car for the weekend., answer as me

Some verbs, like lend and give, can take two objects: the person (indirect object) and the thing (direct object). We put the person straight after the verb, with no preposition.

She gave her sister a necklace for her birthday. · Can you lend me twenty dollars until Friday?

Деякі дієслова (lend, give) мають два додатки: особу і річ. Особу ставимо одразу після дієслова, без прийменника: lent me his car — «позичив мені машину».

give/lend/show/send/pass/teach/write/offer + thing + to + person

She gave the keys ___ (to/for) her brother before she left.

Back: She gave the keys to her brother before she left., answer as to

When the thing comes first, these verbs use 'to' before the person: gave the keys to her brother.

He sent a text message to his colleague. · Could you pass the salt to me, please?

«to» вживаємо з give, lend, show, send, pass, teach, write, offer, коли річ стоїть першою: gave the keys to him («дав ключі йому»).

buy/get/leave/make + thing + for + person

He bought a coffee ___ (to/for) his colleague this morning.

Back: He bought a coffee for his colleague this morning., answer as for

Buy, get, leave and make use 'for' (not 'to') before the person when the thing comes first — you are doing something to benefit them.

She got a snack for her daughter. · I'll leave a note for you on the desk.

«for» вживаємо з buy, get, leave, make — дію робимо «для» когось: bought a coffee for him («купив каву для нього»).

ask & tell: never use a preposition

He asked ___ (I) a difficult question before the meeting.

Back: He asked me a difficult question before the meeting., answer as me

Ask and tell almost always use pattern 1 — verb + person + thing — with no preposition before the person at all.

I told him my name straight away. · Can I ask you a favour?

ask і tell майже завжди без прийменника: asked me a question («запитав мене»), не to me.

the person after the verb is an object pronoun

Could you show ___ (we) your holiday photos?

Back: Could you show us your holiday photos?, answer as us

The person right after the verb must be an object pronoun — me, him, her, us, them — never a subject pronoun like we or I.

She showed him the photos. · Can you give her this letter?

Особа одразу після дієслова — це об'єктний займенник (me, him, us), а не підметовий (I, we, she).

a long person phrase → use pattern 2

We gave a small gift ___ (to) all of the new employees.

Back: We gave a small gift to all of the new employees., answer as to

When the person is described with several words, pattern 2 (thing + to/for + person) sounds more natural than putting the long phrase straight after the verb.

He wrote a message to all the new students. · I sent an invitation to everyone on the team.

Коли особу описано кількома словами, краще pattern 2 (річ + to/for + особа): sent an email to everyone in the office.
