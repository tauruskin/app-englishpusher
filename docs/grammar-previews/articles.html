🔤 Articles

Lesson 4B — 25 questions · 8 cards

Gap Fill

She showed us ___ amazing invention she'd built in her garage.

Answer: an

New information = a/an. Next word starts with a vowel sound = an, consonant sound = a.

My cousin is ___ nurse at the city hospital.

Answer: a

Jobs always take a/an: a teacher, an engineer, a nurse.

I ordered a coffee and a sandwich, but ___ sandwich was cold when it arrived.

Answer: the

Second mention of the same thing = the.

Out of all the runners in the race, she was ___ fastest by far.

Answer: the

Superlative adjective → always 'the': the best, the tallest, the most expensive.

In general, ___ smartphones are far more powerful than computers were twenty years ago.

Answer: –

Talking generally about a whole group = no article: Dogs are loyal. Computers are useful.

He's always been good at ___ basketball, even though he's quite short.

Answer: –

Sports and games = no article: play tennis, like football, enjoy chess.

Error Spot

I need to buy a umbrella before it starts raining.

Correction: an

'Umbrella' starts with a vowel sound, so it needs 'an': 'I need to buy an umbrella.'

I bought a new bike last month but a bike broke down yesterday.

Correction: the

The bike has already been mentioned, so we use 'the': 'but the bike broke down yesterday.'

Everyone agreed that she was a best candidate for the job.

Correction: the

'Best' is a superlative, so it always takes 'the': 'she was the best candidate.'

They're planning a trip along a River Nile next summer.

Correction: the

Rivers always take 'the': 'a trip along the River Nile.'

Her sister just qualified as the dentist after years of study.

Correction: a

A job mentioned for the first time takes 'a/an': 'she qualified as a dentist.'

She is a only person in the office who can speak Japanese.

Correction: the

'Only' always takes 'the': 'She is the only person in the office who can speak Japanese.'

Multiple Choice

Choose the sentence that uses the article correctly:

Answer: The sun was shining brightly all afternoon.

Only one in the whole world/situation = the: the sun, the sky, the internet.

Choose the sentence with the correct article (or no article):

Answer: She has lived in the United Kingdom for ten years.

Most countries = no article. Names with 'Kingdom/States/Republic' or plural endings = the.

Choose the sentence that uses the article correctly:

Answer: Cargo ships cross the Atlantic Ocean every day.

Seas, oceans, rivers = always the.

Choose the sentence that uses the article correctly:

Answer: They own a small vineyard in the south of France.

A region/area within a place = the: the south of Spain, the north of the country.

Choose the sentence that uses articles correctly:

Answer: I usually have a sandwich for lunch and walk to work on foot.

Fixed phrases like at work, for lunch, on foot never take an article.

Choose the sentence someone would actually say out loud:

Answer: My birthday is on the fifth of June.

Spoken dates = the + ordinal number: the third of April.

Choose the sentence that uses the article correctly:

Answer: She was the first woman to climb that mountain alone.

the first / the last / the only — always the, never a/an.

Word Transform

There was ___ (a/the) strange noise coming from the car engine, and nobody knew what it was.

Answer: a

New, unknown thing mentioned for the first time = a/an.

My brother-in-law works as ___ (a/an) electrician for a company in town.

Answer: an

Jobs always take a/an — choose by the sound of the next word.

We finally found the café we'd read about, and ___ (the/a) coffee there was fantastic.

Answer: the

Already mentioned/known = the.

Of all her paintings, this landscape is ___ (the/a) most famous.

Answer: the

Superlative adjective → the.

Their boat finally reached ___ (the/a) Pacific Ocean after three weeks at sea.

Answer: the

Seas/oceans/rivers = always the.

Out of the whole team, Maria was ___ (the/a) only person who finished the marathon.

Answer: the

the only / the first / the last = always the.

Flashcards

a/an — introducing something new

I just adopted ___ energetic puppy from the shelter. as ___

Back: I just adopted an energetic puppy from the shelter., answer as an

Use a/an the first time you mention something, before the listener knows which one. 'Energetic' starts with a vowel sound, so we use 'an'.

She bought a new laptop yesterday. · He works as an architect in Kyiv.

a/an — коли щось згадуємо вперше: an перед голосним звуком, a — перед приголосним.

a/an — jobs and professions

My father is ___ accountant for a big company. as ___

Back: My father is an accountant for a big company., answer as an

We always use a/an before a job title. 'Accountant' starts with a vowel sound, so it takes 'an'.

She's a dentist. · He wants to become an engineer.

Професії завжди з a/an: a teacher, an actor.

the — already mentioned

We watched a documentary last night, and ___ documentary was really moving. as ___

Back: We watched a documentary last night, and the documentary was really moving., answer as the

Once something has been mentioned, both speaker and listener know exactly which one is meant — so we switch to 'the'.

I read a magazine on the train. The magazine was about space. · There's a car outside. The car is blocking our drive.

the — коли вже знаємо, про що йдеться (згадувалося раніше).

the — superlatives

This is ___ biggest shopping centre in the country. as ___

Back: This is the biggest shopping centre in the country., answer as the

Superlative adjectives (biggest, best, most expensive) describe something unique in its group, so they always take 'the'.

He's the tallest player on the team. · That was the worst film I've ever seen.

Найвищий ступінь порівняння завжди з the: the biggest, the best.

the — seas, oceans and rivers

Thousands of boats cross ___ English Channel every year. as ___

Back: Thousands of boats cross the English Channel every year., answer as the

Seas, oceans and rivers always take 'the', even though most place names don't: the English Channel, the River Danube, the Pacific.

They sailed across the Atlantic Ocean. · The River Thames flows through London.

Моря, океани й річки завжди з the: the Pacific, the Danube.

the vs no article — specific time vs general place

We spent ___ whole afternoon walking around town without any plan. as ___

Back: We spent the whole afternoon walking around town without any plan., answer as the

Use 'the' for one specific instance, like 'the whole afternoon' (that particular afternoon). But we say 'around town' with no article when we mean the general idea of being in town, not one specific place.

I grew up in a small town. · She walked around town all day without a map.

the whole afternoon — конкретний момент; around town (без артикля) — загальне поняття.

the — defining expressions

He was ___ last guest to leave the party. as ___

Back: He was the last guest to leave the party., answer as the

Defining expressions like 'the only', 'the first' and 'the last' always take 'the', because they point to one specific, unique thing.

This is the only café open this late. · Who was the first person to arrive?

the only, the first, the last — завжди з the, бо вказують на щось унікальне.

no article — sports, meals and transport

After playing tennis for ___ hour, we went home for dinner. as ___

Back: After playing tennis for an hour, we went home for dinner., answer as an

Sports (tennis, football), meals (breakfast, dinner) and forms of transport (by car, on foot) never take an article. The normal a/an rule still applies elsewhere in the sentence — 'hour' starts with a vowel sound, so it's 'an hour'.

She plays volleyball every weekend. · We usually travel by train.

Спорт, їжа (dinner) і транспорт (by car) — без артикля: play tennis, by car.
