🔗 Relative Clauses

Lesson 5A — 25 questions · 7 cards

Gap Fill

Christopher Blair is the writer ___ invents fake news stories online.

Answer: who

who = people. He's the man who took my bag.

This is the website ___ I read the news every morning.

Answer: where

where = places. This is the building where we got married.

He writes news stories ___ are completely invented.

Answer: which

which = things. This is the bank card which she was looking for.

She remembers the exact moment ___ she realised the story was fake.

Answer: when

when = times. That was the day when I graduated.

That's the journalist ___ article got millions of views overnight.

Answer: whose

whose = possession. Is that the man whose phone you picked up?

This is the headline ___ convinced thousands of people to share a fake story.

Answer: which

which can be the subject of the relative clause too: the thing which did something.

Error Spot

She's the woman which lives in the flat upstairs.

Correction: who

'Woman' is a person, so use 'who', not 'which': 'She's the woman who lives in the flat upstairs.'

This is the book who I borrowed from the library.

Correction: which

'Book' is a thing, so use 'which', not 'who': 'This is the book which I borrowed from the library.'

That's the café who serves the best coffee in town.

Correction: which

The café is the subject doing the action, so use 'which': 'That's the café which serves the best coffee in town.'

He's the man whose took my wallet yesterday.

Correction: who

No possession is shown here — the man is the subject who took the wallet: 'He's the man who took my wallet yesterday.'

Is that the student who's phone keeps ringing in class?

Correction: whose

This shows possession (the student's phone), so use 'whose': 'Is that the student whose phone keeps ringing in class?'

That was the year which I graduated from university.

Correction: when

'Year' is a time, so use 'when', not 'which': 'That was the year when I graduated from university.'

Multiple Choice

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: He's the journalist who writes for the newspaper.

who = people, always.

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: This is the bank card which she was looking for.

which = things, always.

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: This is the building where we got married.

where = places. Think: 'in that place'.

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: That was the day when I graduated from university.

when = times. Think: 'at that time'.

Which sentence is correct?

Answer: Is that the man whose phone you picked up?

whose + noun = possession. who's = who is / who has.

In which sentence can the relative pronoun NOT be replaced by 'that'?

Answer: Is that the man whose phone you picked up?

that = who/which substitute. that ≠ whose, ever.

In which sentence can the relative pronoun safely be left out?

Answer: She's the woman I was telling you about.

Object of the clause → pronoun optional. Subject of the clause → pronoun required.

Word Transform

She's the reporter ___ (person) exposed the scandal.

Answer: who

person → who (or that).

This is the newspaper ___ (thing) published the story first.

Answer: which

thing → which (or that).

That's the café ___ (place) we always meet for coffee.

Answer: where

place → where.

I'll never forget the moment ___ (time) I saw my name in print.

Answer: when

time → when (or sometimes that).

He's the author ___ (possession) new book became a bestseller.

Answer: whose

possession → whose, always followed by a noun.

She's the journalist ___ (person — object of the clause) everyone in the newsroom respects.

Answer: who

Object relative clause: who/that fills the gap, or it can be dropped entirely in natural speech.

Flashcards

who — for people

She's the woman ___ called the ambulance.

Back: She's the woman who called the ambulance., answer as who

We use 'who' for people in a relative clause. It can be the subject, like here (she called the ambulance).

He's the man who works at the bakery on the corner. · They're the tourists who got lost downtown yesterday.

who = «який/яка» про людей: she's the woman who called — вона подзвонила.

which — for things

That's the article ___ caused so much debate online.

Back: That's the article which caused so much debate online., answer as which

We use 'which' for things and animals in a relative clause, never for people.

This is the laptop which stopped working last week. · He deleted the post which upset so many readers.

which = «який/яка/яке» про речі, не про людей: the article which caused debate.

where — for places

This is the café ___ we always meet after work.

Back: This is the café where we always meet after work., answer as where

We use 'where' for places in a relative clause — it means 'in that place'.

That's the park where we had our picnic. · This is the office where I had my first job interview.

where = «де»: café where we meet — кафе, де ми зустрічаємось.

when — for times

I'll never forget the day ___ I got my first job.

Back: I'll never forget the day when I got my first job., answer as when

We use 'when' for times in a relative clause — it means 'at that time'.

That was the summer when we travelled around Europe. · Do you remember the moment when the power went out?

when = «коли»: the day when I got the job — день, коли я отримав роботу.

whose — for possession

He's the man ___ car was stolen last night.

Back: He's the man whose car was stolen last night., answer as whose

We use 'whose' to show that something belongs to the person or thing just mentioned. It is always followed by a noun.

She's the woman whose dog barks every morning. · That's the company whose app crashed yesterday.

whose = «чий/чия/чиє»: the man whose car — чоловік, чия машина.

that instead of who or which

He's the man ___ (who/that) stole my bike.

Back: He's the man who stole my bike., answer as who

In defining relative clauses, 'that' can replace 'who' or 'which' with no change in meaning. Both forms are correct.

This is the bank card that she'd been looking for all day. · She's the journalist that wrote the article.

that часто замінює who/which у визначальних реченнях: who/that — без різниці.

leaving out the pronoun (object clauses)

He's the man ___ (who/that) I told you about.

Back: He's the man who I told you about., answer as who

When the person or thing is the OBJECT of the relative clause (not the subject), we can leave out who, which or that completely: 'He's the man I told you about.'

She's the woman I was telling you about. · This is the book I borrowed from the library.

Коли іменник — об'єкт речення, who/which/that можна взагалі пропустити.
